# Sanctions Exposure

A wallet does not need to appear directly on a sanctions list to have relevant exposure.

Funds move.

Wallets interact.

That creates relationships.

## What exposure can look like

It may be:

- direct
- indirect
- recent
- historical
- one-time
- repeated

Those differences matter.

## Why users care

Some exchanges, custodians and payment companies may review or freeze funds when they see certain kinds of exposure.

That makes sanctions-related context important even for users who are not doing compliance work themselves.

## What to look at

A useful view should answer:

- what entity or address is involved
- how close the relationship is
- when the interaction happened
- whether it was one transaction or a pattern
- which public source supports the label

## What not to conclude

An on-chain relationship does not prove ownership, intent or identity.

"Connected to" is not the same as "controlled by."

That distinction matters.

## ChainZap view

Sanctions data should be shown as evidence with context.

Not as a magic red badge that pretends the whole story is obvious.
